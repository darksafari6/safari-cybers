## Capstone: defend your recovery decision

A senior teammate asks to roll back the application everywhere. Reverting it is technically possible, but your current evidence does not strongly distinguish the two application cohorts. The identity owner can pause their configuration expansion and compare East behavior with the previous approved configuration. You need to propose a next action and explain what would make you change it.

### Make the decision explicit

A reasonable first move is to stop expanding both changes while the identity owner checks the regional difference. Avoid changing both systems simultaneously when there is time to observe one change safely; otherwise you lose the ability to tell what helped. If the impact becomes severe, prioritize a reversible action that restores service and record the uncertainty it leaves behind.

### Deliverable template

1. **Impact:** State the affected workflow, scope, and earliest confirmed time.
2. **Evidence:** List the strongest comparison and at least one limitation.
3. **Decision:** Name the owner and next action; explain why it is proportionate.
4. **Recovery:** Define the signal, scope, and duration that would justify closure.
5. **Follow-up:** Propose two prevention actions, each with an owner and a success criterion.

### Self-review rubric

A strong report separates fact from interpretation, uses the supplied counts accurately, recommends a feasible next step, and chooses recovery metrics that match the original customer complaint. A weak report assigns a cause based solely on timing or uses a green home-page check as evidence that sign-in recovered. Revise until another person could take over using your report alone.
