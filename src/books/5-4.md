## A network incident handoff under pressure

You are covering the morning shift when a fictional store reports that checkout works in one region but not another. Your goal is not to be the first to name a culprit. It is to reduce uncertainty, protect customer impact, and hand over a clear account of what is happening.

### Separate the layers in the report

Write observations in order: name resolution returned an address, the client established a protected connection, and the gateway returned an error on checkout. Product browsing worked. These facts narrow the problem without proving which dependency is failing. If a test reached only the home page, do not cite it as proof checkout works.

### Worked decision

A second region has not yet received the new checkout release and its error rate is lower. Pausing the remaining rollout is reversible and limits exposure while the team checks the comparison. That decision is not a declaration that the release is the root cause. Compare similar traffic, note that traffic mix can differ by region, and name the evidence that would change the plan.

### Your handoff template

1. **Impact:** Which user action fails, where, and since when?
2. **Evidence:** What was observed directly, and at which boundary?
3. **Working explanation:** What might explain the evidence, and what else could fit?
4. **Next action:** Who owns the lowest-risk check or containment step?
5. **Recovery:** What user-facing signal has to improve, and for how long?

### Checkpoint

The checkout error rate falls for two minutes after the pause. Why not declare recovery immediately? Traffic may have dipped, measurement windows may lag, or only one region may have improved. Agree on the recovery window and verify the original workflow in the original affected scope.
